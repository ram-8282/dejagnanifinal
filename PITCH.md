# Déjà — Solution Pitch

Déjà is a voice-first memory companion for people with early-stage dementia or short-term memory loss: they speak about their day, and later ask questions to get their own memories back. Built by Ram and Ritheswar.

## The problem

Daily details such as medicine routines, a doctor's advice or a family visit fade within hours, and with them goes the patient's independence. Journaling apps fail these patients in two ways:

- **Typing is exhausting.** Writing on a screen takes physical and mental effort they no longer have to spare.
- **Search needs memory.** A search bar only works if you remember the right keyword, and that is impossible once the memory is gone.

## The solution

The patient talks, Déjà structures, and the patient asks. Nothing needs typing and no keywords need remembering.

| Module | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **The Slate** | Daily journal. Quick Snippets for short voice notes, Deep Diary for longer reflections. A voice companion asks "Is this for today?" and "Anything else?" |
| **The Oracle** | Answers spoken questions such as "What did Rahul bring me at 4 PM?" using only the patient's own memories. |
| **Blindspots** | Scans past entries for patterns: missed medicines, falls, recurring confusion and stress points. |
| **The Archives** | A vault of every past entry, by date. |

## How it helps a patient day to day

- **"Did I take my tablets?"** Instead of guessing, or taking a second dose, the patient asks the Oracle and hears exactly what they recorded and when.
- **Remembering who visited.** Names, relations and visits are kept: "Who is Arjun?" or "When did Priya last come?" have real answers, which eases the embarrassment of forgetting family.
- **Following the doctor's advice.** Advice spoken after a check-up, like "walk 20 minutes" or "less salt", can be asked about days later, without remembering what it was called.
- **Speaking, not typing.** A warm voice companion guides each entry ("Is this for today?", "Anything else?"), in Indian languages and at a slower pace if needed. Shaky hands or failing eyesight are no barrier.
- **Catching changes early.** Blindspots notices repeated falls, missed medicines, poor sleep or growing confusion. These are signs a family or doctor might otherwise miss until a crisis.
- **A record for doctor visits.** The Archives give the neurologist a dated history instead of a vague "I'm fine."
- **Comfort and identity.** Birthdays, anniversaries and memories of loved ones are kept and can be revisited. That gives reassurance on the bad days.

*Under the hood:* Gnani AI speech-to-text → Cognee knowledge graph of people, places, times and medicines → grounded answers from the Oracle.

## Why it matters

- **Dignity and independence.** The technology adapts to how people speak, instead of patients adapting to complex apps.
- **Grounded answers.** Answers come only from the patient's own memory graph, so it does not guess at things that never happened.
- **Caregiver relief.** Families get an always-available outside memory and early warning from Blindspots.

**Demo:** say "My nephew Rahul came around 4 PM today and brought my hypertension medicine." Then ask "What did Rahul bring me at 4 PM today?" The Oracle returns that exact event.
